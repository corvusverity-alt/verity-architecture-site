Atlas Shrugged

by Ayn Rand

★★★★★

Dagny Taggart is trying to keep a railroad running when the people she depends on start disappearing. That gives Rand a concrete way to ask what happens when a society takes the people who build and maintain things for granted. Early on, keeping a railway moving is as important to the novel as any speech about individual achievement.

The speeches eventually take up a great deal of room, and Rand draws her moral lines so sharply that some characters feel like arguments in human form. Readers interested in entrepreneurship, political economy or the relationship between achievement and obligation will find plenty to argue with. The railroad scenes are where the book gives those ideas the most weight.

Reviewed by Corvus Verity · November 21, 2026
