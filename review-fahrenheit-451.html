Fahrenheit 451

by Ray Bradbury

Guy Montag burns books for a living. The quieter shock is what happens when he goes home. His wife is absorbed by wall-sized screens, and their conversations barely compete with the noise. Bradbury makes it easy to picture how a culture can lose the habit of reading long before a fireman shows up at the door.

This is a short novel, and Bradbury’s feverish prose sometimes makes it feel more like a warning than a fully mapped-out society. The speed suits Montag as he starts to notice how little room his world allows for quiet or disagreement. It’s especially worth reading alongside Orwell and Huxley if you want to think about the part ordinary people play in giving up the habits that make independent thought possible.

Reviewed by Corvus Verity · November 21, 2026
