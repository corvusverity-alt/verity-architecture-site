Canada and Other Matters of Opinion

by Rex Murphy

★★★★★

Rex Murphy can move from Solzhenitsyn to Don Cherry and Tim Hortons without losing the thread of what interests him about Canada. His writing works best in individual pieces, where his ear for rhythm and his willingness to get specific can make even a familiar argument feel worth revisiting.

Some of the political quarrels now belong to their moment, and the force of Murphy’s delivery can at times carry an argument further than its evidence. The Newfoundland pieces offer another side of him, with affection and impatience sharing the same page. It’s a good book to dip into if you’re interested in how a distinctive Canadian commentator made his case, even when you’d make a different one.

Reviewed by Corvus Verity · November 21, 2026
