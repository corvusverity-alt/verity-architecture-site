Sir Henry Pellatt: The King of Casa Loma

by Carlie Oreskovich

★★★★★

I found Pellatt’s story heartbreaking. Here was a man who helped develop Ontario’s early electric power industry and built Casa Loma, a house that still draws people to Toronto. Oreskovich makes you feel the distance between what he built and how little of his fortune he had left by the end.

The sequence matters. Financial trouble forced the Pellatts to leave the castle years before Toronto seized the property for unpaid taxes. That’s what makes it a story about staying power as much as ambition. It reminded me of a question I wrote about in Financially Trapped. How much can you afford to keep when the conditions that let you build it change? For readers interested in Canadian business history, this is a rise and fall worth following.

Reviewed by Corvus Verity · November 20, 2026
