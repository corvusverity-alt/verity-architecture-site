Brave New World and Brave New World Revisited

by Aldous Huxley

Huxley imagines a society that has made obedience comfortable. People are conditioned for their place in the social order, given ready distractions and offered a drug called soma when discomfort breaks through. The harshest moment comes when John claims the right to be unhappy. In a society organized around keeping everyone satisfied, the freedom to grieve or struggle becomes an argument someone has to make.

The companion essays revisit the novel’s concerns through propaganda, persuasion and conditioning. Some of the mid-century examples have aged, but the pairing gives readers something unusual. First you see what it feels like to live in Huxley’s world. Then you can examine how he thought a real society might get there.

Reviewed by Corvus Verity · November 21, 2026
