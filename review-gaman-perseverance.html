Gaman – Perseverance

by Art Miki

★★★★★

The 1988 Japanese Canadian Redress Agreement can fit into a single sentence in a history book. Art Miki shows what it took to get there. His family history and memories of growing up in British Columbia give the negotiations a human scale. By the time the government acknowledges the wrong, readers have a sense of the years Japanese Canadians spent organizing and pressing for recognition.

The memoir also follows Miki into education, community work and later public service. That wider scope sometimes takes the reader away from the redress negotiations, but it shows how the work carried on after the signing. For readers interested in Canadian civil rights, the chapters on redress make clear how much patient organizing sits behind a public apology.

Reviewed by Corvus Verity · November 21, 2026
